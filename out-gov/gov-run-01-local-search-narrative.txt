* Step 0: Initial state

## One-Sentence Summary

A proposed service that detects life events such as the birth of a child, job loss, or retirement and would offer eligible families the relevant government support automatically, without an application.

## Problem

Many families who qualify for social support never receive it because they do not know it exists, the rules are hard to understand, or the application requires documents from several entities. People in difficult moments, such as after losing a job, face the most paperwork at the time they have the least energy for it.

## Target Beneficiaries & Use Cases

Families going through major life events: new parents, people who recently lost their job, new retirees, and people with a newly registered disability. A typical use case is a mother who registers a birth and, within days, would receive a message listing the support she qualifies for, with one tap to accept.

## Solution / Service Overview

The initiative proposes linking existing government records, with the person's consent, so that eligibility could be checked in the background when a life event is registered. The citizen would receive a clear message explaining what they qualify for and why, and could accept, decline, or ask a human case worker for help. No new documents would be requested when the information already exists in government systems.

## Delivery Model

The service would be delivered through the existing government app and SMS, with a phone line and service centers for people who are not digital. A small central team would maintain the eligibility rules, and each participating entity would keep ownership of its own data.

## Funding & Sustainability

The initiative plans to be funded from the existing social support budget, with the aim of offsetting costs through reduced application processing and fewer appeals. A proposed first phase would cover three life events before expanding.

## What Makes It New

Most government services wait for people to apply. This proposal reverses the direction: the government would come to the citizen at the moment of need, which is rare in social services.

## Measuring Impact

Proposed indicators include the share of eligible families receiving support, the time from life event to first payment, and citizen satisfaction. A planned comparison with the current application route would test whether uptake improves.

## Risks & Mitigation

Privacy and consent are the main risks; the initiative proposes an explicit opt-in and a clear record of which data was used. Wrong eligibility decisions would be reduced by keeping a human review step for edge cases.
