# One-Sentence Summary
A proposed government service that proactively detects household utility emergencies by analyzing smart meter data and, for eligible families experiencing major life events, automatically offers a bundled package of support including guided home-based therapies and financial relief, without requiring a formal application.

## Problem
Hidden utility emergencies in homes, such as water leaks, waste large amounts of a critical resource and often go unnoticed until a very high bill arrives, causing severe financial stress and disputes with the utility. These crises create a moment of acute need where residents are least equipped to navigate complex support systems.

## Target Beneficiaries & Use Cases
Families going through major life events that create new support needs: new parents, people who recently lost their job, new retirees, people with a newly registered disability, and patients discharged after orthopedic surgery or a stroke. A specific addition proposed is **informal caregivers**, such as a family member assuming primary responsibility for an elderly relative after a hospital discharge, as these individuals often become the de facto patient support system while also facing new logistical and emotional burdens. The initiative also proposes to explicitly include **households in temporary or insecure housing**, such as those in social housing, emergency accommodation, or on short-term tenancies, as these residents are particularly vulnerable to the financial shock of a utility emergency and may lack the agency or resources to arrange repairs. A typical use case is a household where a hidden leak is detected; because the household is also identified as containing a patient recently discharged after a stroke and is in a temporary housing arrangement, the residents would receive an alert and be automatically presented with a bundled package of support, which could also include resources for the informal caregiver and specific protections against tenancy disruption due to the emergency.

## Solution / Service Overview
The initiative proposes a bundled support package delivered directly to the citizen. For eligible patients recovering from surgery or stroke, the package would include guided physiotherapy at home through scheduled live video sessions with a therapist and daily exercises guided by a simple phone app that uses the camera to count repetitions and flag incorrect movements. The therapist would review progress remotely and adjust the plan. This health support would be automatically bundled with other relevant supports triggered by the same life event and the detected utility emergency, such as financial aid for the unexpected bill, access to subsidized repair services, and caregiver-specific resources like respite care options.

## Delivery Model
The service would be delivered by public hospital departments and relevant social agencies. For the rehabilitation component, devices would be lent to patients who lack a smartphone, with a proposed initial home visit to set up the technology and train a family member if needed. The proactive offer and management of the bundled support package, triggered by the fusion of utility data alerts and life-event data, would be coordinated through a central digital channel, with SMS and a phone line maintained for those not comfortable with digital tools. Each participating entity would keep ownership of its own data.

## Funding & Sustainability
The initiative proposes to be funded within existing hospital and social support budgets, with the aim of lowering long-term costs from reduced hospital readmissions, fewer missed therapies, more efficient application processing, and reduced resource losses (like water) and billing disputes. Savings from these avoided costs are proposed to offset the upfront costs of the technology, service integration, and streamlined support.

## What Makes It New
Most government safety programs issue general warnings and rely on workers or employers to know and claim their rights. This proposal reverses the direction: by linking life-event and utility data (with consent), the system would proactively identify the at-risk household and come to them at the moment of imminent danger or acute need with a pre-configured bundle of specific, actionable protections and supports, which is rare in public service delivery.

## Measuring Impact
Proposed indicators include the share of eligible households receiving support after an alert, completion rates of prescribed rehabilitation plans, improvements in patient mobility scores, the rate of hospital readmissions within 90 days, water saved, and the number of billing disputes avoided. A planned comparison would test whether this proactive, bundled model improves uptake and outcomes and reduces financial stress compared to the current system where citizens must discover crises and seek out each service separately.

## Risks & Mitigation
Privacy and consent are the main risks; the initiative proposes an explicit opt-in for data linkage and a clear record of which data was used for the alert. Wrong eligibility decisions would be mitigated by keeping a human review step for edge cases. To avoid annoyance, detection thresholds for utility alerts would be set conservatively in a proposed first phase.

# One-Sentence Summary
A proposed government service that proactively detects household utility emergencies by analyzing smart meter data and, for eligible families experiencing major life events, automatically offers a bundled package of support including guided home-based therapies and financial relief, without requiring a formal application.

## Problem
Hidden utility emergencies in homes, such as water leaks, waste large amounts of a critical resource and often go unnoticed until a very high bill arrives, causing severe financial stress and disputes with the utility. These crises create a moment of acute need where residents are least equipped to navigate complex support systems.

## Target Beneficiaries & Use Cases
Families going through major life events that create new support needs: new parents, people who recently lost their job, new retirees, people with a newly registered disability, and patients discharged after orthopedic surgery or a stroke. A specific addition proposed is **informal caregivers**, such as a family member assuming primary responsibility for an elderly relative after a hospital discharge, as these individuals often become the de facto patient support system while also facing new logistical and emotional burdens. The initiative also proposes to explicitly include **households in temporary or insecure housing**, such as those in social housing, emergency accommodation, or on short-term tenancies, as these residents are particularly vulnerable to the financial shock of a utility emergency and may lack the agency or resources to arrange repairs. A typical use case is a household where a hidden leak is detected; because the household is also identified as containing a patient recently discharged after a stroke and is in a temporary housing arrangement, the residents would receive an alert and be automatically presented with a bundled package of support, which could also include resources for the informal caregiver and specific protections against tenancy disruption due to the emergency.

## Solution / Service Overview
The initiative proposes a bundled support package delivered directly to the citizen. For eligible patients recovering from surgery or stroke, the package would include guided physiotherapy at home through scheduled live video sessions with a therapist and daily exercises guided by a simple phone app that uses the camera to count repetitions and flag incorrect movements. The therapist would review progress remotely and adjust the plan. This health support would be automatically bundled with other relevant supports triggered by the same life event and the detected utility emergency, such as financial aid for the unexpected bill, access to subsidized repair services, and caregiver-specific resources like respite care options.

## Delivery Model
The service would be delivered by public hospital departments and relevant social agencies. For the rehabilitation component, devices would be lent to patients who lack a smartphone, with a proposed initial home visit to set up the technology and train a family member if needed. The proactive offer and management of the bundled support package, triggered by the fusion of utility data alerts and life-event data, would be coordinated through a central digital channel, with SMS and a phone line maintained for those not comfortable with digital tools. Each participating entity would keep ownership of its own data.

## Funding & Sustainability
The initiative proposes to be funded within existing hospital and social support budgets, with the aim of lowering long-term costs from reduced hospital readmissions, fewer missed therapies, more efficient application processing, and reduced resource losses (like water) and billing disputes. Savings from these avoided costs are proposed to offset the upfront costs of the technology, service integration, and streamlined support. **A proposed addition to the funding model is a targeted partnership with regulated utility companies, where a portion of the demonstrable savings from reduced water loss and avoided billing disputes is directly reinvested by the utilities into the program's operational costs. This would create a sustainable, performance-based funding stream tied directly to the value the initiative creates for the utility sector.**

## What Makes It New
Most government safety programs issue general warnings and rely on workers or employers to know and claim their rights. This proposal reverses the direction: by linking life-event and utility data (with consent), the system would proactively identify the at-risk household and come to them at the moment of imminent danger or acute need with a pre-configured bundle of specific, actionable protections and supports, which is rare in public service delivery. **The proposed initiative would differ meaningfully from existing services by moving beyond a single-agency, single-crisis response to a multi-agency, anticipatory bundling model. It would not just alert a household to a leak and offer a subsidy for repairs, but would, in the same proactive step, integrate support from health, social services, and housing agencies tailored to a detected major life event, creating a unified safety net triggered by a single data point.**

## Measuring Impact
Proposed indicators include the share of eligible households receiving support after an alert, completion rates of prescribed rehabilitation plans, improvements in patient mobility scores, the rate of hospital readmissions within 90 days, water saved, and the number of billing disputes avoided. A planned comparison would test whether this proactive, bundled model improves uptake and outcomes and reduces financial stress compared to the current system where citizens must discover crises and seek out each service separately.

## Risks & Mitigation
Privacy and consent are the main risks; the initiative proposes an explicit opt-in for data linkage and a clear record of which data was used for the alert. Wrong eligibility decisions would be mitigated by keeping a human review step for edge cases. To avoid annoyance, detection thresholds for utility alerts would be set conservatively in a proposed first phase.

A specific addition proposed is **informal caregivers**, such as a family member assuming primary responsibility for an elderly relative after a hospital discharge, as these individuals often become the de facto patient support system while also facing new logistical and emotional burdens. The initiative also proposes to explicitly include **households in temporary or insecure housing**, such as those in social housing, emergency accommodation, or on short-term tenancies, as these residents are particularly vulnerable to the financial shock of a utility emergency and may lack the agency or resources to arrange repairs. A typical use case is a household where a hidden leak is detected; because the household is also identified as containing a patient recently discharged after a stroke and is in a temporary housing arrangement, the residents would receive an alert and be automatically presented with a bundled package of support, which could also include resources for the informal caregiver and specific protections against tenancy disruption due to the emergency.

## Solution / Service Overview
The initiative proposes a bundled support package delivered directly to the citizen. For eligible patients recovering from surgery or stroke, the package would include guided physiotherapy at home through scheduled live video sessions with a therapist and daily exercises guided by a simple phone app that uses the camera to count repetitions and flag incorrect movements. The therapist would review progress remotely and adjust the plan. This health support would be automatically bundled with other relevant supports triggered by the same life event and the detected utility emergency, such as financial aid for the unexpected bill, access to subsidized repair services, and caregiver-specific resources like respite care options.

## Delivery Model
The service would be delivered by public hospital departments and relevant social agencies. For the rehabilitation component, devices would be lent to patients who lack a smartphone, with a proposed initial home visit to set up the technology and train a family member if needed. The proactive offer and management of the bundled support package, triggered by the fusion of utility data alerts and life-event data, would be coordinated through a central digital channel, with SMS and a phone line maintained for those not comfortable with digital tools. Each participating entity would keep ownership of its own data. **The initiative proposes to pilot the delivery of the bundled support package through trusted, place-based intermediaries such as community health centers, public libraries, and social housing resident associations. These intermediaries would act as local hubs where citizens could receive in-person assistance to understand and activate their support package, access the loaned devices, and get help navigating the integrated services, thereby ensuring the digital-first coordination model does not exclude those with low digital literacy or trust.**

## Funding & Sustainability
The initiative proposes to be funded within existing hospital and social support budgets, with the aim of lowering long-term costs from reduced hospital readmissions, fewer missed therapies, more efficient application processing, and reduced resource losses (like water) and billing disputes. Savings from these avoided costs are proposed to offset the upfront costs of the technology, service integration, and streamlined support.

## What Makes It New
Most government safety programs issue general warnings and rely on workers or employers to know and claim their rights. This proposal reverses the direction: by linking life-event and utility data (with consent), the system would proactively identify the at-risk household and come to them at the moment of imminent danger or acute need with a pre-configured bundle of specific, actionable protections and supports, which is rare in public service delivery. **The proposed initiative would differ meaningfully from existing services by moving beyond a single-agency, single-crisis response to a multi-agency, anticipatory bundling model. It would not just alert a household to a leak and offer a subsidy for repairs, but would, in the same proactive step, integrate support from health, social services, and housing agencies tailored to a detected major life event, creating a unified safety net triggered by a single data point. A proposed key innovation is the use of the utility emergency as a 'tracer condition'—a non-medical, objectively measurable crisis that reliably signals a broader moment of vulnerability across multiple life domains, enabling the system to identify households in need of a multi-faceted intervention before they experience cascading failures.**

## Measuring Impact
Proposed indicators include the share of eligible households receiving support after an alert, completion rates of prescribed rehabilitation plans, improvements in patient mobility scores, the rate of hospital readmissions within 90 days, water saved, and the number of billing disputes avoided. A planned comparison would test whether this proactive, bundled model improves uptake and outcomes and reduces financial stress compared to the current system where citizens must discover crises and seek out each service separately.

## Risks & Mitigation
Privacy and consent are the main risks; the initiative proposes an explicit opt-in for data linkage and a clear record of which data was used for the alert. Wrong eligibility decisions would be mitigated by keeping a human review step for edge cases. To avoid annoyance, detection thresholds for utility alerts would be set conservatively in a proposed first phase.

# One-Sentence Summary
A proposed government initiative that proactively detects household utility emergencies through smart meter data and, for eligible families experiencing major life events, automatically offers a bundled package of support including guided home-based therapies, financial relief, and streamlined access to business formalization for caregivers, delivered through trusted local hubs staffed by cross-trained support personnel and funded in part by utility sector savings.

## Problem
Hidden utility emergencies in homes, such as water leaks, waste large amounts of a critical resource and often go unnoticed until a very high bill arrives, causing severe financial stress and disputes with the utility. These crises create a moment of acute need where residents are least equipped to navigate complex support systems.

## Target Beneficiaries & Use Cases
Families going through major life events that create new support needs: new parents, people who recently lost their job, new retirees, people with a newly registered disability, and patients discharged after orthopedic surgery or a stroke. A specific addition proposed is **informal caregivers**, such as a family member assuming primary responsibility for an elderly relative after a hospital discharge, as these individuals often become the de facto patient support system while also facing new logistical and emotional burdens. The initiative also proposes to explicitly include **households in temporary or insecure housing**, such as those in social housing, emergency accommodation, or on short-term tenancies, as these residents are particularly vulnerable to the financial shock of a utility emergency and may lack the agency or resources to arrange repairs. A typical use case is a household where a hidden leak is detected; because the household is also identified as containing a patient recently discharged after a stroke and is in a temporary housing arrangement, the residents would receive an alert and be automatically presented with a bundled package of support, which could also include resources for the informal caregiver and specific protections against tenancy disruption due to the emergency.

## Solution / Service Overview
The initiative proposes a bundled support package delivered directly to the citizen. For eligible patients recovering from surgery or stroke, the package would include guided physiotherapy at home through scheduled live video sessions with a therapist and daily exercises guided by a simple phone app that uses the camera to count repetitions and flag incorrect movements. A proposed addition is to incorporate a digital tool for monitoring and managing medication adherence into the rehabilitation package. This health support would be automatically bundled with other relevant supports triggered by the same life event and any detected utility emergency, such as a single application for business licenses and permits for informal caregivers, access to startup grants, financial aid for unexpected utility bills, access to subsidized repair services, and caregiver-specific resources like respite care options.

## Delivery Model
The service would be delivered by public hospital departments and relevant social agencies. For the rehabilitation component, devices would be lent to patients who lack a smartphone, with a proposed initial home visit to set up the technology and train a family member if needed. The proactive offer and management of the bundled support package, triggered by the fusion of utility data alerts and life-event data, would be coordinated through a central digital channel, with SMS and a phone line maintained for those not comfortable with digital tools. Each participating entity would keep ownership of its own data. **The initiative proposes to pilot the delivery of the bundled support package through trusted, place-based intermediaries such as community health centers, public libraries, and social housing resident associations. These intermediaries would act as local hubs where citizens could receive in-person assistance to understand and activate their support package, access the loaned devices, and get help navigating the integrated services, thereby ensuring the digital-first coordination model does not exclude those with low digital literacy or trust. A proposed addition is to embed dedicated, cross-trained support staff within these local hubs. These staff would be employed by a lead coordinating agency and trained across the domains of health, social services, and utility support, enabling them to provide integrated guidance and troubleshoot issues across the entire bundled package from a single point of contact.**

## Funding & Sustainability
The initiative proposes to be funded within existing hospital and social support budgets, with the aim of lowering long-term costs from reduced hospital readmissions, fewer missed therapies, more efficient application processing, and reduced resource losses (like water) and billing disputes. Savings from these avoided costs are proposed to offset the upfront costs of the technology, service integration, and streamlined support. **A proposed addition to the funding model is a targeted partnership with regulated utility companies, where a portion of the demonstrable savings from reduced water loss and avoided billing disputes is directly reinvested by the utilities into the program's operational costs. This would create a sustainable, performance-based funding stream tied directly to the value the initiative creates for the utility sector.**

## What Makes It New
Most government safety programs issue general warnings and rely on workers or employers to know and claim their rights. This proposal reverses the direction: by linking life-event and utility data (with consent), the system would proactively identify the at-risk household and come to them at the moment of imminent danger or acute need with a pre-configured bundle of specific, actionable protections and supports, which is rare in public service delivery. **The proposed initiative would differ meaningfully from existing services by moving beyond a single-agency, single-crisis response to a multi-agency, anticipatory bundling model. It would not just alert a household to a leak and offer a subsidy for repairs, but would, in the same proactive step, integrate support from health, social services, and housing agencies tailored to a detected major life event, creating a unified safety net triggered by a single data point. A proposed key innovation is the use of the utility emergency as a 'tracer condition'—a non-medical, objectively measurable crisis that reliably signals a broader moment of vulnerability across multiple life domains, enabling the system to identify households in need of a multi-faceted intervention before they experience cascading failures.**

# One-Sentence Summary
A proposed government initiative that proactively detects household utility emergencies through smart meter data and, for eligible families experiencing major life events, automatically offers a bundled package of support including guided home-based therapies, financial relief, and streamlined access to business formalization for caregivers, delivered through trusted local hubs staffed by cross-trained support personnel and funded in part by utility sector savings.

## Problem
Hidden utility emergencies in homes, such as water leaks, waste large amounts of a critical resource and often go unnoticed until a very high bill arrives, causing severe financial stress and disputes with the utility. These crises create a moment of acute need where residents are least equipped to navigate complex support systems.

## Target Beneficiaries & Use Cases
Families going through major life events that create new support needs: new parents, people who recently lost their job, new retirees, people with a newly registered disability, and patients discharged after orthopedic surgery or a stroke. A specific addition proposed is **informal caregivers**, such as a family member assuming primary responsibility for an elderly relative after a hospital discharge, as these individuals often become the de facto patient support system while also facing new logistical and emotional burdens. The initiative also proposes to explicitly include **households in temporary or insecure housing**, such as those in social housing, emergency accommodation, or on short-term tenancies, as these residents are particularly vulnerable to the financial shock of a utility emergency and may lack the agency or resources to arrange repairs. A typical use case is a household where a hidden leak is detected; because the household is also identified as containing a patient recently discharged after a stroke and is in a temporary housing arrangement, the residents would receive an alert and be automatically presented with a bundled package of support, which could also include resources for the informal caregiver and specific protections against tenancy disruption due to the emergency.

## Solution / Service Overview
The initiative proposes a bundled support package delivered directly to the citizen. For eligible patients recovering from surgery or stroke, the package would include guided physiotherapy at home through scheduled live video sessions with a therapist and daily exercises guided by a simple phone app that uses the camera to count repetitions and flag incorrect movements. A proposed addition is to incorporate a digital tool for monitoring and managing medication adherence into the rehabilitation package. This health support would be automatically bundled with other relevant supports triggered by the same life event and any detected utility emergency, such as a single application for business licenses and permits for informal caregivers, access to startup grants, financial aid for unexpected utility bills, access to subsidized repair services, and caregiver-specific resources like respite care options.

## Delivery Model
The service would be delivered by public hospital departments and relevant social agencies. For the rehabilitation component, devices would be lent to patients who lack a smartphone, with a proposed initial home visit to set up the technology and train a family member if needed. The proactive offer and management of the bundled support package, triggered by the fusion of utility data alerts and life-event data, would be coordinated through a central digital channel, with SMS and a phone line maintained for those not comfortable with digital tools. Each participating entity would keep ownership of its own data. **The initiative proposes to pilot the delivery of the bundled support package through trusted, place-based intermediaries such as community health centers, public libraries, and social housing resident associations. These intermediaries would act as local hubs where citizens could receive in-person assistance to understand and activate their support package, access the loaned devices, and get help navigating the integrated services, thereby ensuring the digital-first coordination model does not exclude those with low digital literacy or trust. A proposed addition is to embed dedicated, cross-trained support staff within these local hubs. These staff would be employed by a lead coordinating agency and trained across the domains of health, social services, and utility support, enabling them to provide integrated guidance and troubleshoot issues across the entire bundled package from a single point of contact. A further proposed addition to the delivery model is to establish a formalized peer-support network within these hubs, where experienced beneficiaries who have successfully navigated the bundled support system could be recruited and trained to provide mentorship and practical advice to new recipients, supplementing the professional guidance from cross-trained staff.**

## Funding & Sustainability
The initiative proposes to be funded within existing hospital and social support budgets, with the aim of lowering long-term costs from reduced hospital readmissions, fewer missed therapies, more efficient application processing, and reduced resource losses (like water) and billing disputes. Savings from these avoided costs are proposed to offset the upfront costs of the technology, service integration, and streamlined support. **A proposed addition to the funding model is a targeted partnership with regulated utility companies, where a portion of the demonstrable savings from reduced water loss and avoided billing disputes is directly reinvested by the utilities into the program's operational costs. This would create a sustainable, performance-based funding stream tied directly to the value the initiative creates for the utility sector.**

## What Makes It New
Most government safety programs issue general warnings and rely on workers or employers to know and claim their rights. This proposal reverses the direction: by linking life-event and utility data (with consent), the system would proactively identify the at-risk household and come to them at the moment of imminent danger or acute need with a pre-configured bundle of specific, actionable protections and supports, which is rare in public service delivery. **The proposed initiative would differ meaningfully from existing services by moving beyond a single-agency, single-crisis response to a multi-agency, anticipatory bundling model. A proposed key innovation is the use of the utility emergency as a 'tracer condition'—a non-medical, objectively measurable crisis that reliably signals a broader moment of vulnerability across multiple life domains, enabling the system to identify households in need of a multi-faceted intervention before they experience cascading failures.**

# One-Sentence Summary
A proposed government service that proactively detects household utility emergencies by analyzing smart meter data and, for eligible families experiencing major life events, automatically offers a bundled package of support including guided home-based therapies and financial relief, without requiring a formal application.

## Problem
Hidden utility emergencies in homes, such as water leaks, waste large amounts of a critical resource and often go unnoticed until a very high bill arrives, causing severe financial stress and disputes with the utility. These crises create a moment of acute need where residents are least equipped to navigate complex support systems.

## Target Beneficiaries & Use Cases
Families going through major life events that create new support needs: new parents, people who recently lost their job, new retirees, people with a newly registered disability, and patients discharged after orthopedic surgery or a stroke. A specific addition proposed is **informal caregivers**, such as a family member assuming primary responsibility for an elderly relative after a hospital discharge, as these individuals often become the de facto patient support system while also facing new logistical and emotional burdens. The initiative also proposes to explicitly include **households in temporary or insecure housing**, such as those in social housing, emergency accommodation, or on short-term tenancies, as these residents are particularly vulnerable to the financial shock of a utility emergency and may lack the agency or resources to arrange repairs. A typical use case is a household where a hidden leak is detected; because the household is also identified as containing a patient recently discharged after a stroke and is in a temporary housing arrangement, the residents would receive an alert and be automatically presented with a bundled package of support, which could also include resources for the informal caregiver and specific protections against tenancy disruption due to the emergency.

## Solution / Service Overview
The initiative proposes a bundled support package delivered directly to the citizen. For eligible patients recovering from surgery or stroke, the package would include guided physiotherapy at home through scheduled live video sessions with a therapist and daily exercises guided by a simple phone app that uses the camera to count repetitions and flag incorrect movements. The therapist would review progress remotely and adjust the plan. This health support would be automatically bundled with other relevant supports triggered by the same life event and the detected utility emergency, such as financial aid for the unexpected bill, access to subsidized repair services, and caregiver-specific resources like respite care options.

## Delivery Model
The service would be delivered by public hospital departments and relevant social agencies. For the rehabilitation component, devices would be lent to patients who lack a smartphone, with a proposed initial home visit to set up the technology and train a family member if needed. The proactive offer and management of the bundled support package, triggered by the fusion of utility data alerts and life-event data, would be coordinated through a central digital channel, with SMS and a phone line maintained for those not comfortable with digital tools. Each participating entity would keep ownership of its own data. **The initiative proposes to pilot the delivery of the bundled support package through trusted, place-based intermediaries such as community health centers, public libraries, and social housing resident associations. These intermediaries would act as local hubs where citizens could receive in-person assistance to understand and activate their support package, access the loaned devices, and get help navigating the integrated services, thereby ensuring the digital-first coordination model does not exclude those with low digital literacy or trust. A proposed key addition to this model is the establishment of a dedicated, cross-agency **Service Navigation Corps**, comprising trained public servants from health, social services, and housing agencies. These navigators would be embedded within the proposed local hubs and would provide in-person, end-to-end case management for the most complex eligible households, ensuring the bundled supports are activated, coordinated, and adapted over time, especially for beneficiaries facing multiple vulnerabilities.**

## Funding & Sustainability
The initiative proposes to be funded within existing hospital and social support budgets, with the aim of lowering long-term costs from reduced hospital readmissions, fewer missed therapies, more efficient application processing, and reduced resource losses (like water) and billing disputes. Savings from these avoided costs are proposed to offset the upfront costs of the technology, service integration, and streamlined support.

## What Makes It New
Most government safety programs issue general warnings and rely on workers or employers to know and claim their rights. This proposal reverses the direction: by linking life-event and utility data (with consent), the system would proactively identify the at-risk household and come to them at the moment of imminent danger or acute need with a pre-configured bundle of specific, actionable protections and supports, which is rare in public service delivery. **The proposed initiative would differ meaningfully from existing services by moving beyond a single-agency, single-crisis response to a multi-agency, anticipatory bundling model. It would not just alert a household to a leak and offer a subsidy for repairs, but would, in the same proactive step, integrate support from health, social services, and housing agencies tailored to a detected major life event, creating a unified safety net triggered by a single data point. A proposed key innovation is the use of the utility emergency as a 'tracer condition'—a non-medical, objectively measurable crisis that reliably signals a broader moment of vulnerability across multiple life domains, enabling the system to identify households in need of a multi-faceted intervention before they experience cascading failures. It is proposed that this model would be tested as a blueprint for anticipatory government, where a single, non-intrusive data signal from one domain (like utilities) is used to unlock a pre-authorized, cross-domain support package, potentially applicable to other tracer conditions such as a sudden drop in energy consumption signaling an elderly person's health decline or a missed rent payment flagged by a public housing provider.**

## Measuring Impact
Proposed indicators include the share of eligible households receiving support after an alert, completion rates of prescribed rehabilitation plans, improvements in patient mobility scores, the rate of hospital readmissions within 90 days, water saved, and the number of billing disputes avoided. A planned comparison would test whether this proactive, bundled model improves uptake and outcomes and reduces financial stress compared to the current system where citizens must discover crises and seek out each service separately.

## Risks & Mitigation
Privacy and consent are the main risks; the initiative proposes an explicit opt-in for data linkage and a clear record of which data was used for the alert. Wrong eligibility decisions would be mitigated by keeping a human review step for edge cases. To avoid annoyance, detection thresholds for utility alerts would be set conservatively in a proposed first phase.
