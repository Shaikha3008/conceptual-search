# UAE air quality brief

## 1. Main pollutants and comparison with WHO guidelines

- In November 2023, the Environment Agency – Abu Dhabi (EAD) named particulate matter and ground-level ozone as Abu Dhabi's main pollutants of concern. NO2 and CO were below UAE limits. SO2 was within limits at most stations but had risen in the Al Dhafra Region. [11]
- The 2021 WHO guidelines set annual means of 5 µg/m³ for PM2.5, 15 µg/m³ for PM10 and 10 µg/m³ for NO2. The 24-hour PM2.5 value is 15 µg/m³, and the peak-season ozone value is 60 µg/m³. [8]
- IQAir reported a UAE average PM2.5 of 43 µg/m³ in 2023 and 33.7 µg/m³ in 2024, a 22% fall. [7] For 2025, IQAir gives 36.4 µg/m³, which is 7.3 times the WHO annual guideline. [6]
- HEI/IHME modelling puts population-weighted PM2.5 at 44 µg/m³ in 2019 (range 33–58 µg/m³). It is a modelled figure, while IQAir uses monitor data, so the two do not match exactly. [15]
- An Abu Dhabi study published in 2026 found maximum PM10 of 218 µg/m³ and maximum PM2.5 of 60 µg/m³ in summer. [10] A 2013 study reported that PM2.5 and PM10 in Abu Dhabi frequently exceed guideline and limit values. [9]

## 2. Main sources

- The government lists dust and sandstorms as natural sources. It lists fuel combustion, transport, energy production and industry as human sources. [2]
- EAD's 2018 Air Emissions Inventory names oil and gas, road transport and industry as Abu Dhabi's biggest contributors. NOx and PM peak near heavy traffic and in the Musaffah and Kizad industrial areas. SO2 and NMVOCs peak in oil and gas areas and near ports. [12]
- PM2.5 was measured at two Abu Dhabi sites in 2012. Long-range transport of human-made pollution was the largest source (~40%), and mineral dust came second (~25%). Traffic, industry and shipping were also significant. [9]
- The 2026 study found a PM2.5/PM10 ratio below 0.5 and named desert dust as the main driver of PM. [10]
- Dubai's air quality improved 17.7% in 2020 while COVID-19 restrictions cut traffic, flights and industry. [20]

## 3. Climate factors

- Surface ozone forms in daytime from NMVOCs and NOx. It correlates strongly with temperature and peaks in July and August. [17]
- The authors of a 2010 study linked ground-level ozone of 45–50 ppbv to pollution, strong solar radiation and swings in humidity. [18]
- Data from 1983–2014 show dust storms peak in winter, and most dust events fall in March and April. About 90% of dust episodes come from the southwest. [19] By contrast, PM concentrations in Abu Dhabi peak in summer because of dust events. [10]

## 4. Health and economic impact

- A peer-reviewed study estimated about 650 deaths in 2008 from ambient air pollution (95% CI 140–1,400). [14]
- HEI/IHME estimates that air pollution caused nearly 12% of UAE deaths in 2019, or 104 per 100,000 people against a global average of 86. [15]
- Economist Impact estimated in 2025 that meeting WHO guidelines could save about US$596 million and about 4,000 lives a year. [16]

## 5. Policies, programmes and infrastructure

- **Standards.** Cabinet Decree No. 12 of 2006 sets federal ambient limits but has no separate PM2.5 standard. [4]
- **National Air Quality Agenda 2031.** The Cabinet approved it in June 2022 and MOCCAE launched it in September 2022. It rests on three pillars: monitoring, mitigation and management. Outdoor air has 8 goals and 29 action themes covering transport, energy, construction and waste. [1][3] Reported targets include 100% compliance with gaseous standards and 90% with a PM2.5 standard, both by 2040. [4]
- **Monitoring.** The National Air Quality Platform publishes a real-time AQI for five pollutants. [2] MOCCAE reclassified 64 stations in 2023. [5] EAD runs 20 fixed and 2 mobile stations. [13] Dubai Municipality had more than 72 fixed stations in 2020. [20] EAD launched an air quality modelling and forecasting system in 2023. [11]
- **Fuel.** Diesel must contain 10 ppm sulphur and meet the Euro 5 standard. [21]
- **Electric vehicles.** The 2023 National EV Policy targets EVs making up 50% of vehicles by 2050 and plans a national charging network. [22]
- **Net Zero 2050.** The initiative was announced in October 2021. [23]
- **Nuclear.** The Barakah plant was completed in 2024. It supplies up to 25% of UAE electricity and avoids 22.4 million tonnes of CO2 a year. [24]
- **Transit.** Dubai Metro carried 275.4 million riders in 2024. [25] Etihad Rail's 900 km freight network took more than 500,000 truck trips off the roads in 2024, and passenger services are planned for 2026. [26]
- **Green buildings.** Abu Dhabi has required at least 1 Pearl (Estidama) for new developments since 2010. [28] Dubai's Al Sa'fat has been mandatory at Silver level for new buildings since October 2020. [27]

## 6. Gaps and challenges

- There is no federal PM2.5 standard. [4]
- In 2021, 83% of days in Abu Dhabi met AQI limits, short of the 90% target. [12][13]
- In 2019, 62% of inspected Dubai companies were still using off-specification diesel. [21]
- SO2 is rising in Al Dhafra. [11]
- Source apportionment studies are scarce. [9]
- Health estimates vary widely between studies. [14][15]
